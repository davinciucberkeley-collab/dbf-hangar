## Summary
A variant of the scratch tilt-tricopter (K06) in which the two front motors sit on one tube that a single servo rotates through the wing, so it needs one tilt servo instead of two. It is a mechanism option rather than a new layout: it saves about $28 against K06, but the one servo becomes a single point of failure and the differential-tilt yaw is in doubt. The screening merges it into K06 and leaves the choice to a bench rig.

## Key facts
- Both front motors sit on one carbon tube that runs through the wing, and one servo rotates that tube. [MORPH.md §3.3 row U05]
- Halves the tilt servos relative to K06; the source's reference design (HEEWING T1) uses two. [MORPH.md §3.3 row U05, Source column]
- Cost is K06 minus about $28. [SCREENING.md, screening table row U05]
- The single tilt servo is a single point of failure. [MORPH.md §3.3 row U05, What kills it column]
- Bench test: a tube driven by one servo, carrying two motors at hover thrust, swept from 0 to 90 degrees; under $40 and one day (ESTIMATE); passes if the servo holds and does not strip at full thrust. [MORPH.md §5 test table, row U05]
- ArduPilot's binary tilt type suits retract-style servos; the audit log files it under the tilt rows as a mechanism option, not a separate entry. [AUDIT-LOG.md, evidence table row K06 / K07 / U05 tilt servos]

## Status in the study
MERGE->K06: a mechanism option on K06 that the bench rig will decide. [SCREENING.md, row U05]

## Open questions
- The source marks the ArduPilot tilt-tri's use of differential tilt for yaw as UNVERIFIED, so the yaw loss from dropping it is not confirmed. [MORPH.md §3.3 row U05]
- The bench-rig cost and time are ESTIMATE. [MORPH.md §5 test table, row U05]
