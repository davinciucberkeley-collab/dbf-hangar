## Summary
A tailsitter quad with outer wing panels that fold or slide inward while hovering, aimed at wind drift, which the source names as the tailsitter's main weakness. The only support for the gain is a paper; the one folding-wing prototype the team could find, from UPenn, was never flown with its wings out. The screening keeps it as a phase-2 option on K09, to be added only if measured gust drift at the field demands it.

## Key facts
- The outer panels retract or fold while hovering, which also yields a packed transport state. [MORPH.md §3.3 row U06]
- The cited paper reports gust drift falling from 0.820 m to 0.081 m at about 5 m/s. [MORPH.md §3.3 row U06, Why column]
- The only source for the concept is an arXiv paper (2511.04251); no build was found. [MORPH.md §3.3 row U06, Source column; SCREENING.md, row U06]
- The UPenn bistable folding-wing quad (ICRA 2021) snaps between folded and deployed with no extra actuator; its 783 g prototype had its wings tested by hand and never flew winged. [AUDIT-LOG.md, evidence table row U06]
- Screened cost is K09 plus hinges and servos, about $60, which adds one or two fold mechanisms under aero load and 2 to 3 weeks. [SCREENING.md, row U06]

## Status in the study
MERGE->K09 (phase-2 option): adds a loaded mechanism and weeks, so build K09 first and add the fold only if measured gust drift at the field demands it. [SCREENING.md, row U06]
