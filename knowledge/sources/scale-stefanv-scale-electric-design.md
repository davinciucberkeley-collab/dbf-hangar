## Summary
A magazine-style walk-through that chooses the scale factor from the motor power available, then derives target weight, wing area, speeds and prop pitch from simple power laws. Worked example is a 1/8 to 1/7.6 scale light plane. It says nothing on tail sizing, stability or CG.

## Usable rules / numbers
- Scale factor from power: s = cube root of (model output power / full-size power); 135 W against about 60 000 W gives about 1/7.6 (section on choosing scale).
- Lengths scale by s, areas by s^2, weight and power by s^3, wing loading by s, speed by sqrt(s) (scaling-rules list; 1/8 example: 27 ft span to about 40.5 in, about 31 oz, about 117 W, stall about 14 mph, cruise about 42 mph).
- Power loading (W/lb) is the same at every scale (Zodiac 60 W/lb carries over).
- Input power = output power / motor efficiency; current = input power / pack voltage (power section).
- Prop pitch speed target about 2.5 to 3 x stall speed; pitch speed [mph] = pitch [in] x rpm x 0.000947 (propeller tuning steps).
- Reynolds number falls with size; small models behave differently (limits section).

## How we could use it
- Reference-aircraft template: RC-scale fields (scale factor, target mass = full-size MTOW x s^3, power, stall speed).
- Cross-check: if the scale-weight is below what real parts weigh, the scale factor is too small (see recon 2026-11-24-real-aircraft-to-rc-model).
