## Summary
University fluid-mechanics notes, section 9.10, with a worked example of the wind force on a flat plate. The example takes the drag coefficient of a rectangular flat plate normal to the flow from a drag-coefficient table, and states the plate's height-to-width ratio. It does not reproduce the table or give values for other shapes.

## Usable rules / numbers
- [wind_drag_coefficient] For a rectangular flat plate normal to the flow with H/W = 1.75, the example uses c_D = 1.2, obtained "from a drag coefficient table for a flat plate" (worked example liftdrag_01, PDF p. 4; page footer 997). Point value, single shape.
- [wind_drag_coefficient] Example geometry: W = 4 in, H = 7 in, area 0.19 ft^2, speed 55 mph (80.7 ft/s). Hydraulic-diameter Re about 2.2e5 at 55 mph, stated as not used in the calculation (PDF p. 4).

## How we could use it
- Gives one bluff-body reference point (1.2, flat plate normal, H/W 1.75, Re about 2e5) for wind_drag_coefficient. It is not a shape-general airframe value.
