### Judgment on the stall reference (KCAS 53/48 kt instead of the brief's 48/40 kt)
The brief set `VS1 = 48 kt`, `VS0 = 40 kt` (the KIAS arc limits, POH PDF p.41) and expected +30.7 % / +56.8 % with implied CLmax 1.88. The builder used the same handbook's stall table KCAS values at 2,550 lb (POH PDF p.124: flaps up 48 KIAS / 53 KCAS, flaps 30 40 / 48), giving +18.4 % / +30.7 % and CLmax 1.54, and wrote the reason in the test (`VS1 = 53 * KT`, comment "the 48 is KIAS") and in the findings header.
Accepted. `T3.stall` computes V = sqrt(2W / (rho S CL)) with sea-level rho, an equivalent airspeed, which equals calibrated airspeed at these speeds; indicated airspeed differs by the airspeed-system position error, largest near stall (5 kt here). Comparing with KIAS would put an instrument error into the calibration of a formula (scorecard, Evidence and closure: "Compare a copied or reproduced value with its source"). CLmax 1.54 is the plausible flaps-up value for this wing; 1.88 is not. The brief's hand values were the defect; no stop rule covers it and the builder stayed inside the 5 files.
For the conductor: the plan's U18.2 Step 1 note says "default-CLmax stall error +30.7 % pinned as a finding"; the pinned flaps-up error is now +18.4 % (+30.7 % is against Vso).

### Can a retry double-apply a partial in-place mutation?
No. Both fills are idempotent on the same dict:
- **`fill_swept_poses`** rebuilds `by_file` from the current `cut_poses` and **assigns** `["matrices"] = e["matrices"]`, so it replaces rather than appends. Static `matrix` entries are kept, and `gp["cut_poses"]` is reassigned as a whole. It also catches every `Exception` itself, so it never triggers a retry. Its input, `swept_cut_poses(data, ...)`, depends on the components' meshes and joints, not on the `cut_poses` or `mass_g` written by a first attempt.
- **`fill_generated_mass`** **assigns** `c["mass_g"]`; nothing is summed into an existing value. Volumes come from the same params, so a second pass gives the same numbers.
- **Errors:** `errors[cid] = ...` is also an assignment.

The only residue a failed first attempt can leave is an error entry for a component that succeeds on the retry (a transient failure). That entry is stale but harmless, and today's caught exceptions are deterministic (non-blocking 1). Exceptions the fill does not catch (anything other than GeometryError, KeyError, TypeError or ValueError) now re-raise on every read and re-run the fill each time. That is correct and visible, but repeated work on a persistent failure.

### Judgment: the 9 T1.attachment FAILs on the c172 scratch (wall 3)
Not a Part A bug. They are a Part B layout task.
- **What the check measures.** `T1.attachment_location` uses `checks._point_distance`: 0 when the joint point is inside the part's material, otherwise the distance to the nearest surface of the mesh (`signed_distance`). For a hollow part, "material" is the 3 mm skin, and the nearest surface of a point in the void is the inner skin. `T1.attachment_contact` measures the gap between the two meshes. Both measure against the material the part actually has, not a void.
- **Why these joints fail.** The outer surface is unchanged (probe above), so a joint whose point lies on or in the outer skin still reads 0. All nine failing joints have their point deep inside the old solid foam, where the cabin is now empty:

- **Why that is correct.** A real hollow fuselage has the same problem: the wing saddle, the tail mount, the motor mount and trays for the battery, ESC and receiver must exist as material, and the joint points must lie on it. Measuring to the inner skin is the right physics.
