## Summary
An aerodynamic study of the C0 baseline, a twin-boom quadplane with four lift rotors, a pusher and a NACA 2415 wing. It combines XFOIL and NeuralFoil section polars, an AVL whole-aircraft lattice model, a component drag build-up and a 2-D RANS cross-check. The v0.3.1 geometry failed the stall and endurance requirements mainly because of bare round landing legs; the v0.4 redesign, with faired legs and a re-sized wing, closes them. The tail is a conventional twin-fin H-tail, so the tail cell is the closest fit.

## Key facts
- v0.3.1 (MTOW 6.182 kg, wing span 2.4 m, area 0.5728 m2): trimmed 3-D CL_max is 1.13 against 1.2 assumed in sizing, which gives a stall speed of 12.4 m/s against the 12 m/s limit of requirement R11. [aerodynamics.md verdict table, section 5]
- v0.3.1 total parasite drag coefficient is 0.081 against 0.059 assumed; the four bare round legs (25.4 mm tubes) alone add 0.036. Cruise at 20 m/s then needs 479 W and lasts 9.1 min, below the 10 min of R10; with faired legs (estimate) it is 299 W and 14.6 min. [aerodynamics.md sections 7-8]
- v0.4 values: wing area 0.682 m2, chord 284.2 mm, aspect ratio 8.45, MTOW 6.552 kg, trimmed CL_max 1.114, stall 11.75 m/s (2.1 % margin to 12), CD0 0.0411 with faired legs, cruise L/D 9.48 at 18 m/s, 239 W electrical, 17.3 min of cruise. [aerodynamics.md v0.4 update table]
- Neutral point with the pod is 41.1 % MAC in v0.3.1 (47.3 % without); the pod shifts it forward by 6.2 % MAC. Static margin is 13.1 % at the 28 % CG and 5.2 % at the no-payload CG of 36 %; v0.4 gives 11.8 % and 5.1 %. [aerodynamics.md section 4, v0.4 table]
- The adopted 2-D CL_max is 1.30 from published wind-tunnel data (XFOIL 1.36, NeuralFoil 1.28). The tail section NACA 0009 stalls near 8 degrees at Re 6e4 to 1e5, which limits elevator authority at 12 m/s. [aerodynamics.md section 3]
- The SU2 2-D RANS run is a cross-check only and feeds no sizing number: 28 of 29 cases usable, lift within about 3 to 9 % of XFOIL at 2 and 10 degrees, grid convergence index indicative only. [aerodynamics.md section 6]
- Measurements the team is told to take: watt-meter at 18 m/s (above 420 W fails R10; 10 min needs 436 W or less), stall test at MTOW (above 12.5 m/s fails R11), stopped-rotor drag with and without props, CG weighing, gear-fairing A/B test. [aerodynamics.md section 10]
- Sources cited by DBF-Combined, not opened by this import: XFOIL 6.99 and AVL 3.52 (MIT Drela pages), NeuralFoil and AeroSandbox (Sharpe, GitHub), SU2 8.5.0, gmsh 4.15.2, Genc, Lock and Kaynak (AIAA ATIO 2008), Genc, Karasu and Acikel (Exp. Thermal Fluid Sci. 2012), Raymer 5th ed., Hoerner "Fluid-Dynamic Drag" (1965), Nelson "Flight Stability and Automatic Control", Celik et al. (2008). [aerodynamics.md sections 1, 3, 7]

## Key facts

## Key facts
- The baseline wing predicts stall at exactly 12.00 m/s. If the true trimmed maximum lift coefficient is 5, 10 or 15 percent lower, stall becomes 12.31, 12.65 or 13.02 m/s and R11 fails in every case. [stall-margin.md verdict, section 3]
- Recommended: design stall target 11.75 m/s (4.1 percent lift-coefficient margin), at no cost, keeping the pack and the $103.21 reserve; it adds 88 g and shortens cruise from 12.92 min to 12.35 min, which stays over the 11.5 min floor. [stall-margin.md verdict]

## Summary
A finite-element thermal study of the frozen C0 design (run of 2026-11-02, 18 m/s cruise) covering the four lift motors with their ESCs, the battery and flight-controller bays and the pusher motor in its printed cup. Film coefficients come from textbook correlations rather than CFD, and the loss fractions are estimates. At the 25 C design ambient every component passes, with the lift motor, pusher and pack setting that ceiling.

## Key facts
- Design ambient is 25 C, the full-mission ceiling of requirement R21; each temperature moves one-for-one with ambient, which means a 30 C day pushes the pusher 1 K past its limit. The three limiting components have margins of +5 K (lift motor), +4 K (pusher) and +7 K (pack). [thermal.md verdict]
- Lift unit in continuous hover from 25 C: motor can reaches 44, 59, 73 and 85 C at 30, 60, 90 and 120 s, crossing the estimated 80 C limit near 108 s. In the mission it reaches 59 C after the 60 s take-off hover and reaches 75 C by the finish of the 20 s transition. [thermal.md 6.1]
- Pod: the battery warms about 6.9 K in take-off hover and ends near 53 C at touchdown; the flight-controller board reaches 60 C under a sealed canopy, still rising at touchdown. Battery heat is 26.0 kJ over the mission, 6.9 percent of the pack energy drawn. [thermal.md 6.2, 2]
- Pusher: steady cruise settles near 77 C on the can, 76 C after 600 s, with a time constant of about 153 s; its 33.2 W loss is an estimate stacked on an estimate. [thermal.md 6.3]
- Recommendations: add ram-air inlet and exit slots for the battery bay (two openings of about 15 by 30 mm, estimated), accept packs only at or below 6.0 mohm per cell, keep hover to 60 s or less per rule, move each ESC to the boom side or top, vent the avionics bay, and keep carbon-nylon for motor mounts and the pusher cup (printed PETG mounts would pass their heat deflection point at about 82 s of hover). [thermal.md 6.4]
- Prop wash at the disc is 7.13 m/s induced velocity (thrust 18.23 N per unit, 17 in disc); air properties come from Incropera and DeWitt, 6th edition. [thermal.md section 3]
- Sources cited by DBF-Combined, not opened by this import: Incropera and DeWitt "Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer" 6th ed., NOAA NCEI 1991-2020 climate normals (Berkeley), SOLIDWORKS Simulation 2025. [thermal.md sections 1, 3]

none

- Loss fractions (lift motor 20 percent, ESC 4 percent, pusher 15 percent times 75 percent) are not from datasheets; every temperature rise scales linearly with them.
- Radiation, bay-air warming, solar load and simplified geometry are left out; results are optimistic for the sealed flight-controller bay.

## Summary
A 2026-11-01 analysis of the dimensional fits of the printed hardware in the C0 aircraft, assuming a Prusa MK4S-class printer (the repo does not name the printer). It concludes the printed hardware as drawn will not assemble on the first try: six fits fail, five are borderline, and three tolerance stack-ups need corrective action. No morphological cell applies because the file is about manufacturing fits, not configuration.

## Key facts
- Carbon and aluminium tube tolerance is much coarser than machined fits: a nominal 25.4 mm tube spans 25.197 to 25.603 mm, which is a coarse IT13-class tolerance (330 micrometres) against 21 micrometres for a standard grade-7 fit at 22 mm. The real tube lots must be measured. [tolerance-analysis.md section 2]
- Clamp default of a 25.4 plus 0.2 mm CAD bore prints at 25.30 to 25.55 mm, so the clamp halves bottom out on the smallest tube; the fix is a bore of 25.0 mm (or measured diameter minus 0.22 mm) with the split plane up. [tolerance-analysis.md section 5, 7]
- Clamp preload: a 0.25 N m torque on an M3 bolt gives about 417 N and 13.7 MPa under the washer (21.9 MPa at 0.4 N m); printed PETG relaxes about 2 percent after 1 hour, 12 percent after 1 day, 30 percent after 1 week and 50 percent after 30 days (extrapolated beyond the 22 h tested). [tolerance-analysis.md section 5]
- A heat-set insert in printed PLA resists roughly 1400 N of pull-out; insert bores should be 4.0 to 4.2 mm, 7.5 mm deep. [tolerance-analysis.md sections 5, 7]
- CAD changes listed: ten items covering clamp bores, saddle hole, insert bores, nut pockets, motor-mount screw pockets, tail tab slot, elevator hinge gap, axle block, splice plug and placeholder patterns. [tolerance-analysis.md section 7]
- Calibration coupons are printed first in PETG (about 90 min, estimated), then on the real tubes, then repeated in carbon-nylon. [tolerance-analysis.md section 8]
- Sources cited by DBF-Combined, not opened by this import: Smith's dimensional-accuracy page, CNC Kitchen on heat-set insert data, a University of Florida makerspace tolerance guide, a PETG creep paper, and other items keyed S1 to S13. [tolerance-analysis.md section 9]

none

- The printer is assumed, and process capability numbers are assumed values.
- Motor 19 mm square hole pattern and servo ear pitch are placeholders to measure before cutting.
- The creep time unit is assumed to be seconds (estimate).
- Measure each tube lot, the clamp free gap (below 0.15 mm does not grip), bolt torque after 24 h and 7 days (over 30 percent loss means moving to carbon-nylon clamps), and receipt dimensions for hubs and patterns.
