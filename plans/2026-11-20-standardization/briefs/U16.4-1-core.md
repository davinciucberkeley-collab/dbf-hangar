## Part A: both clevises ride the rod; the doubled pin is not drawn

Real behaviour: both clevises are on the rod ends and move with the rod. Also two pins show at the follower end: the component `<prefix>-pin` and the 5 mm clevis pin in `_clevis_mesh`.

Decision (fixed, do not revisit): keep the clevis meshes as they are (barrel + 5 mm pin, so the picture is the same at both ends); hide the 1.2 mm pin COMPONENT in the visual scene only. The pin stays a component, so mass, CG, T1 interference, MJCF, STL and every validate row stay unchanged.

## Part B: overlay guard in `core/hangar/geom/overlay.py::_evaluate`
After the existing `fit` check and before `return {**st, "state": "present"}, m`, add: if `library.load_part(part_id)` has `frames.output` with `point` and `axis`, compute `ext = max over m.vertices of (v . a)` with `a` the unit axis, and `want = point . a`. If `abs(ext - want) > OUTPUT_END_TOL_MM` (module constant `OUTPUT_END_TOL_MM = 2.0`, a drawing/placement tolerance, not a gate threshold) return `{**st, "reason": f"overlay output end is {abs(ext-want):.1f} mm from frames.output along its axis; check the matrix"}, None` (state stays "unusable"). No `frames` or no `output` key: skip the guard. Put the `st.update(output_end_off_mm=...)` only inside the unusable return; the present-state dict gets no new key (existing tests compare it).

## Part C: add-linkage keeps extra keys on elements it replaces in place
`core/hangar/ops/linkage.py` nested `patch` (line 236-253) raises "cannot be replaced in place (other keys present)" when the old element has keys the new one lacks. After a hand-added `connection: CT-SERVO-SPLINE` on a `<prefix>-servo-arm` attachment this blocks every re-run. Change: the call `patch(old, element, key)` at the end of `put` passes `keep_extra=True`; in `patch`, when `keep_extra` is true and `new` and `old` are dicts (top level only), the check `set(old) - set(new)` is skipped and extra keys of `old` are left untouched. Nested dicts (like `articulation`) and the list-length check behave exactly as today (`keep_extra` is not passed down). `_norm(old) == _norm(element)` early return must compare only the keys of `element` (so an identical re-run with an extra key present still writes nothing): compare `{k: old.get(k) for k in element}` normalised with `_norm(element)`. The op summary string stays as is.
