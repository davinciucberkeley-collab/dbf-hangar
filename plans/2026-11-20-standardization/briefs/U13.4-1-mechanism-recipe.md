## Step 2: `docs/mechanisms.md` (new, about 60 lines; sections in this order, plain text, no new claims beyond what the fixture and the code show)
1. **What this is for.** A control surface needs a servo, an arm on the servo output, a pushrod and a horn; T6 (`T6.mechanism`, `T6.ratio`) only runs when a `mechanisms[]` entry joins them.
2. **The recipe** (numbered): (a) put the servo just ahead of the hinge line (smaller x on a tail or wing trailing surface), on the side of the fixed surface away from the airflow if possible, glued with `embed_ok` where it sits in a recess; (b) the servo arm is a `plate` (`plane xy`, flat on the servo output) joined to the servo by an attachment `servo-arm` with `articulation: {type: revolute, pivot: <spline axis point>, axis: [0, 0, 1], range_deg: [...]}` (the driver); (c) the pushrod is a `strut` from the arm tip to a point just short of the horn; (d) the horn is a `plate` (`plane xz`) glued to the control surface, hanging toward the rod; (e) the surface hinge is an attachment with `articulation` (revolute, pivot on the hinge line) from the fixed surface to the control surface; (f) the rod is joined to the arm by an attachment with `kinematic_link: true` (the driver end); (g) the horn end of the link is a small `pin` component fully inside the horn, joined to the horn by an ordinary attachment (the follower end); the rod is NOT attached to the horn or pin (an attachment from rod to horn would close a loop and T1 refuses articulated joints in a loop); (h) the `mechanisms[]` entry names the driver attachment, the driven hinge, the input range and `link: {driver_end, follower_end}`.
3. **The numbers T6 checks.** `T6.ratio` measures the small-angle ratio from the two link points (`ratio = -(arm lever) / (horn lever)` here: arm tip 6 mm off the servo axis, horn pin 15.25 mm from the hinge line, 6 / 15.25 = 0.393; the sign follows the two axes). Do not guess `expect.ratio`: validate once, read `declared ... geometry gives X` in the `T6.ratio` message, and write X rounded to two decimals; the tolerance is `gate-thresholds.yaml#mechanism_ratio_tol`.
4. **Common failures** (one line each, with the exact message start): `T1.attachment_overlap ... set embed_ok if intended` (arm on the servo, horn on the surface); `T1.attachment_contact: parts are N mm apart` (move the part or the joint location to touch within `attach_tolerance_mm`); `T1.articulation: joint lies in a loop of attachments` (a rod attached at both ends: remove the follower attachment of the rod); `T6.ratio ... dead point: link.driver_end and link.follower_end coincide`; `T6.mechanism ... collide at input` (the rod or horn sweeps through a surface: shorten the horn or move the rod); `link.driver_end ... must be kinematic_link: true with parent and child on the driver side`.
5. **Worked example.** The design in `tests/fixtures/mechanism-recipe/plane.yaml` (checked by `tests/test_mechanism_recipe.py`): copy its six components and six attachments as the pattern, with the numbers for a tail 60 mm deep and an elevator 30 mm deep. Create the parts with `plane design add <path> components --value '{...}' --why "..."` and `plane design add <path> attachments --value '{...}' --why "..."`, and the mechanism entry with `plane design add <path> mechanisms --value '{id: ..., driver: ..., ...}' --why "..."` when a `mechanisms` list already exists, otherwise `plane design set <path> mechanisms --kind add --value '[{...}]' --why "..."`.
6. **Warnings.** `T6.undriven.<joint>` (an articulated joint no mechanism drives) and, for conventional fixed-wing designs, `T6.servo_undriven.<servo>` (U13.4-2): a servo no mechanism uses.
