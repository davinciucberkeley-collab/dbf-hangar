## What is wrong (reviewer, W34-R3 section 18, reproduced on the branch)
On c172-rc the generated linkage gives 15-25 T1 FAIL rows each. Causes, each measured: the horn strut starts at the hinge pivot P and clips the fixed surface; the arm starts at the servo shaft point O inside the servo case (95-99 % embed vs 35 %); the pin is centred inside the 4 mm horn chord (49-50 % embed); the rod starts in the arm plane inside the case (servo-rod 1-6 mm^3) and ends where the horn is. The hand-made c172 linkages pass T1 because: the horn roots on the control-surface skin; the arm sits on its own plane at the servo face; the pin is a tube beside the horn; the rod stops 2 mm short of the pin and lies in the arm plane.

## What exists for pockets (checked on main and the branch)
No generic pocket op. U16.2 `cut_files` pockets exist only for `wing_panel` and mesh files; they do not cut a fuselage or a strut. The only mechanism for a fuselage is the schema 0.3 `components[].cavities` (axis-aligned boxes, design data). **This brief cuts no pocket**: the op builds the arm and rod in one plane at a given distance from the servo; Part B (designs) keeps or moves the existing fuselage cavities to that plane (numbers below).

## Options (replace the ones of the original brief)

## Construction (replaces original steps 1-4; step 5 onward unchanged except the keys named at the end). All in the resolved design, body frame, mm. Round to 4 decimals only when writing.
Inputs as before: `O`, `a_o` (servo output frame, unit), `ax` (unit hinge axis), `P = P0 + S * ax`, surface component `S_id` = child of the hinge attachment. Meshes: `component_parts(component, design.base_dir)` from `hangar.geometry` (import inside the function; no top-level trimesh import) for the servo (`parts_s`, rotation included) and for `S_id` (`parts_f`).
1. Arm plane. `h_top = max(0, max over all vertices v of parts_s of (v - O) . a_o)` (for the ES08MA II this is 1.5: the gearbox top is 1.5 above `O`). Arm pivot `Op = O + (h_top + T/2 + ARM_GAP) * a_o` (2.85 above `O` for that servo). The arm is a strut centred on the plane through `Op` normal to `a_o`.
2. Horn direction (as before): `d = (O - P)` minus its part along `ax`; error if `|d| < 1e-9` (existing text); `n = unit(ax x d)`; flip so `n . unit(horn_dir) >= 0`; error `give --horn-dir` when `abs(n . unit(horn_dir)) < 0.3` (existing text, must still be raised first).
3. Horn root on the skin. `u = unit(n x ax)`, flipped so `u . (cen - P) >= 0` with `cen` = mean of all vertices of `parts_f`. `Ph = P + horn_offset * u`. Cast the ray `Ph + s*n` against every mesh of `parts_f` (`m.ray.intersects_location([Ph], [n], multiple_hits=True)` returns locations, ray index, triangle index; needs `rtree`, installed): the hits with `s > 1e-6` and `m.face_normals[tri] . n > 0` (the ray leaves the solid); `s_k` = the smallest such `s`. None: error `horn root point [x, y, z] is not inside <S_id>; lower --horn-offset or move --station` (contains `horn root`). `K = Ph + s_k * n` (the point on the skin).
4. Pin. `c0 = unit(e_x - n_x * n)` (`e_x = (1,0,0)`; use `(0,1,0)` if the norm is below 1e-6); `q = unit(n x c0)` (this is the thickness direction of the horn strut, the same construction as `geom/airframe._segment`). For a sign `sg` in {+1, -1}: `off = sg * T * q` (half horn thickness plus pin radius); skin-to-pin distance `s = horn_height` if given, else `((Op - K - off) . a_o) / (n . a_o)` if `abs(n . a_o) >= 0.3` (the pin lands in the arm plane), else `DEFAULT_SKIN_TO_PIN`; `H = K + s*n`; `pin0 = H + off`.
   Stage A: `sg = 0`: `H_A`, then `tip_A` by formula 5 with `pin0 = H_A`. `sg = +1` if `(tip_A - H_A) . q >= 0` else `-1` (the pin sits on the side the rod comes from). Stage B: recompute `s`, `H`, `pin0` with that `sg`. When `horn_height` is None and the arm plane formula was used, error if `s < MIN_SKIN_TO_PIN` or `s > MAX_SKIN_TO_PIN`: `the servo arm plane is {s:.2f} mm from the horn skin point along the horn (needs {MIN} to {MAX} mm): move the servo, turn it (rotation_deg) so its shaft points to the horn side, or give --horn-height` (contains `arm plane`).
5. Arm tip (as the original step 2 with `Op` for `O` and the pin of step 4): `v = pin0 - Op`; `w = v - (v . a_o) a_o`; `W = |w|`; error when `W <= arm_length` (existing text, contains `arm length`; check it for stage A and stage B); `phi = acos(arm_length / W)`; `e = cos(phi) * w/W + side * sin(phi) * (a_o x w/W)`; `tip0 = Op + arm_length * e`. Then `(pin0 - tip0) . (tip0 - Op) = 0`, and `(pin0 - Op) . a_o = 0` when the arm plane formula was used.
6. Elements (all `type: other`, ids `<P>-arm`, `-rod`, `-horn`, `-pin`; `diameter 1.5`; `position [0,0,0]`; mass and manufacturing as the original step 3). `rh = unit(pin0 - tip0)`.
   arm: strut `Op` -> `tip0`, chord 4. rod: strut `tip0` -> `pin0 - ROD_SHORT * rh`. horn: strut `K - ROOT_EMBED * n` -> `H + HORN_EXT * n`, chord 4. pin: strut `pin0 - (PIN_LEN/2) * q` -> `pin0 + (PIN_LEN/2) * q`.
   Attachments: `<P>-servo-arm` {parent SERVO_ID, child arm, bolted, location `Op`, articulation {revolute, pivot `Op`, axis `a_o`, range_deg [-servo_range, servo_range]}}; `<P>-arm-rod` {location `tip0`, hinge, kinematic_link true}; `<P>-horn-mount` {parent `S_id`, child horn, glued, location `K`}; `<P>-horn-pin` {parent horn, child pin, bolted, location `pin0`}; all with `embed_ok: true`, `rated_load_n: 10` as before. Mechanism as the original step 5 (the link ends are `<P>-arm-rod` and `<P>-horn-pin`, so the gate sees `tip0` and `pin0`).
7. `data` keeps `components, attachments, mechanism, ratio, tip0, pin0 (= pin centre), rod_length (= |pin0 - tip0|), min_transmission_deg, rows, changed` and adds `arm_pivot` (`Op`), `horn_root` (`K`), `skin_to_pin` (`s`). Rows: unchanged rule (T0 FAIL, T1 FAIL naming the new ids, `T6.*.<P>-drive`).

## Why these formulas (so you do not "simplify" them)
`h_top`: the servo case top is not at `O` (gearbox block 1.5 higher). `ARM_GAP 0.6`: at 0.1 the aileron rod collides with its own servo at input -20 (2.0 mm^3), 0.6 clears. Horn root on the skin and `horn_offset 6`: the horn chord (4) must clear the fixed surface behind the hinge. Pin plane = arm plane: the rod then stays in the plane of the arm and under the servo-to-stab gap; the lever is the pin height below the hinge (ratio = arm length / that height, as the hand-made -0.56). Pin on the rod side, 1.5 beside the horn centre line: no embed, no rod-horn clip.
